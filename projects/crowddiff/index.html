CrowdDiff

Generate several possible crowd maps and combine their head detections to reduce counting errors in dense or ambiguous scenes.

Co-author

FiguresHow it worksResultsPresentationPaper ↗

Figures

How it works

  1. Train an image-conditioned denoising model on noisy density maps made with narrow kernels. A regression branch supports feature learning during training.

  2. Sample multiple density-map hypotheses and convert them into crowd maps containing estimated head locations.

  3. Order the maps by structural similarity and fuse their detections, rejecting duplicate locations using a neighborhood-based radius. Count the resulting head locations.

Multi-hypothesis density estimation

CrowdDiff models a crowd density map as a conditional diffusion output. Repeated sampling produces multiple plausible maps, whose head-location estimates can be fused to improve counting.

The method uses narrow density kernels to retain localized head structure. Its fusion procedure adds new head detections and rejects repeated locations across hypotheses.

Results

Sampling more density maps reduces counting error and increases runtime. MAE is the average absolute error in the estimated number of people.
