Improve crowd counting with multiple predictions.

The question

The challenge.

Dense crowds contain occlusions, ambiguous head locations and background structure. A single density prediction cannot express every plausible interpretation.

The approach

The central idea.

Sample several detailed density maps, convert them into head-location hypotheses, and fuse complementary detections while rejecting duplicate locations.

Measured impact

The results.

Sampling more candidate crowd maps reduces counting error but increases runtime. Explore that tradeoff below. MAE is the average absolute error in the estimated number of people.

Behind the result

How it works.

  1. 01

    Train an image-conditioned denoising model on noisy density maps made with narrow kernels. A regression branch supports feature learning during training.

  2. 02

    Sample multiple density-map hypotheses and convert them into crowd maps containing estimated head locations.

  3. 03

    Order the maps by structural similarity and fuse their detections, rejecting duplicate locations using a neighborhood-based radius. Count the resulting head locations.

Multi-hypothesis density estimation

CrowdDiff models a crowd density map as a conditional diffusion output. Repeated sampling produces multiple plausible maps, whose head-location estimates can be fused to improve counting.

The method uses narrow density kernels to retain localized head structure. Its fusion procedure adds new head detections and rejects repeated locations across hypotheses.

Research contributions

  • Applied conditional diffusion to crowd density estimation.
  • Used a regression branch during training to improve feature learning.
  • Combined multiple density-map hypotheses to improve counting under ambiguous visual conditions.
