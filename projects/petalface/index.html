The question

The challenge.

A high-quality gallery and a degraded probe occupy different visual domains. Full fine-tuning can weaken a strong pretrained recognition model.

The approach

The central idea.

Keep the backbone frozen and train two low-rank adapter branches. Weight their contributions with an image-quality estimate for each input.

Measured impact

The results.

The adapters improve face verification compared with the pretrained model and full fine-tuning in this test. TAR is the proportion of genuine matches accepted at a fixed false-accept rate.

Adaptation BRIAR TAR at FAR 0.1%
Pretrained 45.93%
Full fine-tuning 29.68%
PETALface 55.35%

WACV 2025, Table 2. Swin-B, WebFace4M, BRIAR Protocol 3.1.

Higher TAR is better. Only 0.48% of parameters are trained by PETALface.

Evaluation context

The comparison uses BRIAR Protocol 3.1 and differs from the FG 2025 BRIAR study. Full fine-tuning reaches 29.68% under this protocol.

Behind the result

How it works.

  1. 01

    Keep the pretrained backbone frozen and attach two trainable low-rank adapter branches.

  2. 02

    Estimate input image quality and use it to weight the two branches.

  3. 03

    Train the adapters for low-resolution recognition and evaluate across low-, high- and mixed-quality datasets.

Quality-weighted low-rank adaptation

PETALface adapts a frozen face-recognition backbone using two low-rank adapter branches. An image-quality estimate weights their contributions for each input, allowing adaptation to low-resolution faces while retaining the pretrained representation.

Quality-dependent weighting accounts for the mismatch between clear gallery images and degraded probe images by adapting the contribution of each branch to the input.

Research contributions

  • Adapted a frozen face-recognition backbone with only 0.48% trainable parameters.
  • Weighted two LoRA branches using image quality to support mixed-resolution inputs.
  • Presented as an oral paper at WACV 2025.
