The question

The challenge.

Several spatial controls can describe different parts of a scene, or disagree at the same location. Naively combining controllers can discard important details or weaken one condition.

The approach

The central idea.

Use spatial feature variance as a signal of conditioning intensity. Fuse aligned intermediate features so pretrained controllers can contribute where they carry useful information.

Measured impact

The results.

Evaluation context

Lower FID does not imply better adherence on every conditioning metric. Pose-plus-depth MSE-D is worse than ControlNet in the reported comparison.

Behind the result

How it works.

  1. 01

    Run off-the-shelf conditional models, including ControlNet or T2I-Adapter, for the requested spatial conditions.

  2. 02

    Measure variance in their aligned intermediate features to identify the strength of each condition across spatial locations.

  3. 03

    Fuse the conditional features and use them to guide text-to-image generation with multiple simultaneous controls.

Variance-guided feature fusion

MaxFusion combines intermediate features from pretrained conditional diffusion models. It uses feature variance as a signal of conditioning intensity, allowing different spatial controls to contribute where they are informative.

The models already know how to respond to their individual conditions. Feature fusion lets a generation use several controls without training a new controller for every combination.

Research contributions

  • Composed features from pretrained conditional diffusion models without retraining.
  • Used feature variance to guide the combination of simultaneous conditions.
  • Handled complementary and conflicting controls with off-the-shelf ControlNet and T2I-Adapter models.
